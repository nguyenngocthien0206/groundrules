# DR-011: Sell-through has no value when net received is not positive, and new stores are flagged

## Context
DR-009 settled what "received" means: everything that arrived minus what
the store sent on. It never said what to do when that number is zero or
negative, and nobody asked, because every store in the golden questions
had been trading for months.

The first dashboard export asked about every store and month. NW030
opened on 1 September 2026. It received 590 units in September and sent
1,012 to other stores, so DR-009's formula gave 8,621 / -422 = -20.43, a
number that means nothing. In August, before it opened, it received its
opening stock and sold nothing, so the formula gave 0: true by the
arithmetic, misleading to anyone comparing stores. An agent asking the
semantic layer got the same two numbers.

## Decision
`sell_through_rate` keeps DR-009's formula: base units sold (gross of
returns) / (base units received from suppliers and other stores - base
units transferred out), all in the same period
(`dbt/models/semantic/metrics_supply_chain.yml`). Two rules are added:

1. **No value when net received is zero or negative.** The ratio is
   returned empty, never 0 and never negative. An answer says the store
   took in no stock overall in the period.
2. **New stores are flagged.** A store in scope that opened after the
   period began, and no more than 7 days after it ended, adds the caveat
   `new_store:<store>`. The chain delivers opening stock in the week
   before a store opens, so that stock counts as received while the
   store's sales cover only part of the period, or none of it.

## Options considered
- **Empty value plus a new store caveat (chosen):** the number stays true
  to the formula where it means something, and every answer that mixes in
  a new store says so, for agents and dashboards alike.
- **Hide new stores on the dashboard only:** simpler, but an agent would
  still answer "0" for NW030 in August with nothing to warn the reader.
  Rejected by the Head of Supply Chain.
- **Leave out opening stock from units received:** removes the 0, but the
  opening stock would then never count in any period, so a new store's
  sell-through would read too high for months.
- **Clamp at zero:** hides a negative ratio but turns "no stock came in"
  into "nothing sold", which is a different statement.

## Consequences
- The dashboard shows a blank, not -20.43, for NW030 in September.
- Chain and category answers that include a store opened in or right
  after the period carry `new_store` caveats. GQ-SELL-02 (chain, August)
  and GQ-SELL-03 (respiratory, July and August) now name NW029 and NW030;
  their numbers did not move.
- The 7-day window is an assumption about how the chain opens stores. If
  opening stock starts arriving earlier, this record has to change.
- `sell_through_rate` moves to version 2 and is certified again.

## Worked example
All in base units.

NW030, opened 2026-09-01:
- August: received 36,154 (opening stock, delivered 31 August), sold 0,
  sent away 0. Sell-through = 0 / 36,154 = **0**, with caveat
  `new_store:NW030` (opened 1 day after the period, within 7).
- September: received 590, sent away 1,012, sold 8,621 (data through
  11 September). Net received = 590 - 1,012 = -422, so there is **no
  value**. Its POS data lags, so the answer also carries
  `pos_sync_lag:NW030` (DR-004).

NW029, opened 2026-08-18:
- August: received 36,174 from suppliers (35,294 of it on 17 August) and
  30 by transfer, sent away 1,270, sold 11,870. Net received = 34,934;
  sell-through = 11,870 / 34,934 = **0.340**, with caveat
  `new_store:NW029`: two weeks of sales against all of its opening stock.

NW014 (open all year) is unchanged: 53,782 / 48,152 = **1.117** in August.
