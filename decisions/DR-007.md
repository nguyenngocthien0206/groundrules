# DR-007: Stock and units sold are counted in the base unit

## Context
A tablet SKU is bought by the box, stored by the box in the ERP's receipt
records, and sold by the box, the strip or the single tablet. Vendor A's
POS writes `BOX`, `STRIP`, `TAB`; vendor B writes `Hộp`, `Vỉ`, `Viên`; the
ERP keeps on-hand in tablets but receipts in boxes. Adding the quantity
column as it comes adds boxes to tablets.

The error is not small. One store's daily "units sold" could swing by ten
times depending on whether customers bought strips or loose tablets that
day, and stock compared against sales in different units gave nonsense
cover figures.

## Decision
Every quantity in the marts and every unit metric is in the SKU's **base
unit**: tablet for tablet SKUs, bottle for syrups and sprays, piece for
devices and personal care. Staging maps the three unit vocabularies to the
ERP's codes, and the marts multiply by the ERP's `factor_to_base`
(`qty_base` columns). This applies to `available_stock`,
`sell_through_rate` and `days_of_cover`, and to every hidden unit metric
they use.

## Options considered
- **Base unit (chosen):** the only unit every sale and every batch can be
  converted to without fractions.
- **Purchase unit (box):** what buyers think in, but a strip is a fraction
  of a box, so sales would need decimals and rounding rules.
- **Keep the unit as a dimension and never add across units:** honest, but
  no metric could answer "how many do we have" for a SKU.

## Consequences
- Unit metrics are comparable across stores, vendors and days.
- Numbers look large to buyers used to boxes. Dashboards can divide by
  `purchase_uom_factor` from `dim_sku` for display; the metric itself stays
  in base units.
- A new unit code at a POS vendor needs a mapping in staging. The
  accepted-values tests on `uom_code` fail until it is added.

## Worked example
Store NW001, SKU 100059 "Loratadine 10mg (Box 3x10)", business date
2026-09-03. A box holds 3 strips of 10 tablets (30 tablets).

| Unit sold | Quantity | Tablets per unit | Base units |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Box | 2 | 30 | 60 |
| Strip | 7 | 10 | 70 |
| Tablet | 9 | 1 | 9 |

- Units sold that day: 60 + 70 + 9 = **139 tablets**.
- Adding the quantities as they come gives 2 + 7 + 9 = 18 "units": wrong
  by a factor of almost eight.
