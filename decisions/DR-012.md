# DR-012: A transfer is proposed from the nearest store within 30 minutes that can spare whole boxes, and supply chain approves it

## Context
Days of cover (DR-010) tells a store manager they are about to run out.
It does not say who has stock to give. On the snapshot morning, 211
store-SKUs had under 7 days of cover, 59 of them none at all. For 145 of
those, another store within 30 minutes' drive had more than 60 days of
the same SKU and a whole box to spare. Transfers happened, but by phone, between managers who knew
each other, and nobody could say afterwards why one store was asked and
not another.

The chain wants an agent to find these cases and propose the transfer.
That only works if "who should give" is a rule, not a phone call. Two
things made the rule harder than it looked. Districts are the wrong
distance: Riverside sits on both banks of the river, and NW025 in
Riverside is 7 minutes from NW011 in New Town but 21 minutes from NW023,
another Riverside store. And stock moves in the boxes and
cartons it was bought in, so the arithmetic in base units is not the
quantity anyone can ship.

## Decision
Encoded in `actions/propose_transfer.yml`. All amounts in base units
unless stated; pace and cover as in DR-010 (base units sold over the 28
full days before the as-of day), available stock as in DR-001 and DR-003.

1. **Target:** a store-SKU with cover under 7 days, including a store that
   has run out. A store-SKU with no sales in the 28 days has no cover and
   is never a target or a donor.
2. **Donor:** a store-SKU with cover over 60 days that can give at least
   one whole purchase unit (the SKU's `PURCH_UOM`, a box or a carton) and
   still keep 30 days of cover.
3. **Distance:** the donor is within 30 minutes' drive of the target, by
   the logistics provider's route table (`fct_store_routes`, donor to
   target), never by district. A store with no route yet has no donor and
   is no donor.
4. **Which donor:** the nearest one that still has a unit to spare. One
   donor per proposal.
5. **Quantity:** whole purchase units. Round up to bring the target to 14
   days; never more than the donor can spare; never so much that the
   target ends above 60 days, because it would then be a donor itself. If
   even one unit takes the target above 60 days, there is no proposal. A
   proposal that brings the target to less than 14 days is allowed and
   says so (partial).
6. **Shared donors:** when several targets want the same donor's stock,
   the target with the lowest cover goes first, then by store code. Each
   proposal takes its units from the donor's spare stock; a later target
   goes to its next nearest donor, or gets no proposal.
7. **Excluded on both sides:** stores whose POS data lags (DR-004), whose
   ERP stock has not seen all their sales; and stores opened on or after
   the first day of the 28-day pace window, whose pace is not settled.
8. **Only available stock moves.** The transfer request carries
   `min_days_to_expiry: 90`, so the warehouse picks lots first to expire
   among those with at least 90 days left. A proposal names a quantity,
   not lots.
9. **Approval:** a person in supply chain operations approves each
   proposal. Nothing reaches the ERP without that approval.

## Options considered
- **Route time within 30 minutes (chosen):** the distance a driver
  actually covers. 28 of the 108 proposals at the snapshot take stock
  from another district; in 12 of them a donor in the target's own
  district was also within 30 minutes, only further away.
- **Same district only:** easy to explain, and the way the phone calls
  went. Rejected by the Head of Supply Chain: a store on a district's
  edge is often nearer the next district than its own.
- **Whole purchase units (chosen):** the ERP and the warehouse move boxes
  and cartons; nobody opens a box of 100 tablets to send 51.
- **Exact base units:** closer to the need on paper, but it means opening
  boxes at the donor and repacking. Rejected.
- **Partial from the nearest donor (chosen):** one van, one stop. The
  proposal says how far it gets the target.
- **The nearest donor that can cover the whole need:** fewer partial
  proposals, but longer drives, and on the snapshot data another donor
  within 30 minutes could cover the whole need in only 7 of the 28
  partial cases.
- **No proposal past 60 days (chosen):** a slow seller that has run out
  is better reordered from the supplier than sent a carton that lasts a
  year. Without the cap, 35 proposals would have made the target a donor
  on the spot.
- **Execute without approval:** the agent would move stock on its own.
  Rejected: an agent proposes, a person decides, and the decision is on
  record.

## Consequences
- At the snapshot (2026-09-15) the rule gives 108 proposals, 94 in boxes
  and 14 in cartons. 28 are partial. 21 go to stores that had run out.
- 66 targets have no donor within 30 minutes. 35 are left out by the
  60-day cap, all of them stores that had run out, 22 of them SKUs that
  sold 4 units or fewer in 28 days: those go back to supplier reorders.
  2 targets lose their only donor to a more urgent target (NW016 and
  NW021).
- No donor ever drops below 30 days; the lowest after a proposal is 30.7.
  No target ends above 60; the highest is 56.0.
- The 7, 14, 30, 60 day and 30 minute numbers are supply chain's
  assumptions. Changing any of them is a new record and a new version of
  the action, like a metric.
- The action reads `available_stock` v3 and `days_of_cover` v1. When either
  changes version, the lint fails until supply chain re-checks this rule
  against the new definition and bumps the pin.
- An agent must say when a proposal is partial, and why a store gets no
  proposal (no donor in reach, the 60-day cap, or excluded store).

## Worked example
All in base units; sold = base units sold 2026-08-18 to 2026-09-14.

**A full proposal.** NW013 (Airport), SKU 100056 Cetirizine 10mg (Box
10x10, 100 tablets): available 103, sold 1,229, so 43.89 a day and 2.35
days of cover. 14 days is 614.5; it needs 511.5 more, 5.1 boxes, rounded
up to 6. Nearest donor NW009 (Airport, 13.9 minutes): available 1,067,
sold 213, 7.61 a day, 140.3 days. Keeping 30 days (228.2) leaves 838.8
to spare, 8 whole boxes. Proposal: **6 boxes (600 tablets)**. NW013 goes
to 703, 16.0 days; NW009 to 467, 61.4 days.

**A partial proposal.** NW014 (Old Quarter), SKU 100025 Celecoxib 200mg
(Box 10x10): available 38, sold 1,176, 42 a day, 0.90 days. It needs
588 - 38 = 550, 6 boxes. The only donor within 30 minutes, NW003 (6.3
minutes): available 357, sold 90, 3.21 a day, 111.1 days. Keeping 30 days
(96.4) leaves 260.6, 2 whole boxes. Proposal: **2 boxes (200)**, partial.
NW014 goes to 238, 5.67 days; NW003 to 157, 48.8 days.

**Nearest is in another district, and shared.** SKU 100146 Sensitive
toothpaste 100g (box of 24). NW011 (New Town) has available 105, sold 17,
172.9 days; keeping 30 days (18.2) leaves 86.8, 3 boxes to spare.
- NW025 (Riverside): available 13, sold 131, 2.78 days, needs 3 boxes.
  Its nearest donor is NW011, 6.9 minutes away in New Town; the only
  Riverside donor, NW023, is 21.0 minutes away. NW025 has the lower
  cover, so it goes first: **3 boxes (72) from NW011**, to 18.2 days.
  NW011 keeps 33, 54.4 days.
- NW022 (New Town): available 18, sold 81, 6.22 days, needs 1 box. Its
  nearest donor, NW011 (19.5 minutes), has nothing left to spare, so it
  goes to the next one, NW023 (26.7 minutes; available 32, sold 5, 1 box
  to spare): **1 box (24) from NW023**. NW022 goes to 42, 14.5 days;
  NW023 to 8, 44.8 days.

**No proposal: the 60-day cap.** NW001 (Old Quarter), SKU 100125 Kids
multivitamin syrup 100ml (carton of 24): available 0, sold 2. Six stores
within 30 minutes could spare a carton, the nearest NW003 at 11.2
minutes. But one carton is 24 bottles, 336 days at 2 bottles in 28 days,
and 60 days is 4.3 bottles: not even one carton fits. **No proposal.**
