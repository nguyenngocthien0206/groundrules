# DR-006: Revenue is reported before VAT

## Context
Shelf prices in Vietnam include VAT, and both POS systems record amounts
the way the customer paid them. Store managers think in those prices. The
chain's VAT is not its money: it is collected for the tax authority, and
the rate differs by category (medicines at one rate, supplements and
personal care at another in this example). Revenue with VAT therefore
changes with the product mix, not only with sales.

## Decision
Revenue metrics are reported **before VAT**. Each amount is divided by
(1 + the SKU's VAT rate) in `int_sales_lines` and `int_return_lines`;
`net_revenue` and `gross_margin` use only the `_excl_vat` columns. Nothing
is rounded in the marts; how to round is left to reports.

## Options considered
- **Before VAT (chosen):** matches the books and the tax return, and makes
  margins comparable across categories.
- **With VAT:** matches the till and store managers' intuition, but mixes
  tax into revenue and makes a shift to higher-VAT products look like growth.

## Consequences
- Store managers see lower numbers than their till totals. Store reports
  label revenue "before VAT".
- VAT rates live in the ERP SKU master. A rate change applies from the day
  it is changed in the ERP; history keeps the rate in force for each line
  only if the ERP keeps it, which is a known limit of this example.
- Cost is recorded before VAT too, so margin compares like with like.

## Worked example
Sales line A-A0012607100046-1 at store NW001 on 2026-07-10: 3 strips of
SKU 100083 at 18,000 VND each (VAT 5%), loyalty discount 2,700 VND.
- Gross with VAT: 3 x 18,000 = 54,000. Paid with VAT: 54,000 - 2,700 = 51,300.
- Paid before VAT: 51,300 / 1.05 = **48,857.14 VND**. This is what the line
  adds to `net_revenue`.
- Gross before VAT 51,428.57 and discount before VAT 2,571.43 give the same
  result: 51,428.57 - 2,571.43 = 48,857.14.
