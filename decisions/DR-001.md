# DR-001: Available stock excludes batches under 90 days to expiry

## Context
"How much do we have?" got three answers. The ERP shows everything on the
shelf, including lots that expire next month. Store managers counted what
they would actually hand to a customer. Replenishment counted what they
could still move to another store before it went bad. A store could look
fully stocked while most of its units were weeks from expiry.

Short-dated stock is not free stock. A customer on a 30-day course should
not get a box that expires in three weeks, and suppliers only take
short-dated lots back if the return is booked well before expiry. So the
line between "sellable" and "at risk" is a business rule, not a data fact.
The marts only carry `days_to_expiry`; this record sets the line.

## Decision
- `available_stock` counts on-hand units only in batches with **90 days or
  more** to expiry at the snapshot (filter `days_to_expiry >= 90` on
  `available_on_hand_units`, `dbt/models/semantic/fct_inventory_batch.yml`),
  then subtracts stock in transit (DR-003).
- `near_expiry_value` is the cost value of batches with **fewer than 90
  days** to expiry, **including expired stock** not yet written off (filter
  `days_to_expiry < 90`). The two metrics split the shelf along the same line.

## Options considered
- **90 days (chosen):** leaves time to sell to short-course customers, to
  transfer stock to a busier store, or to book a supplier return. Asked for
  by the Responsible Pharmacist.
- **60 days:** more units count as available, so fewer reorders. Asked for
  by Retail Operations. Rejected because returns to suppliers need about a
  month to book and ship, and a transfer plus a month of sales does not fit
  in 60 days for slow sellers.
- **Count everything, flag short-dated stock separately:** simplest for the
  data team, but every consumer would have to remember to subtract the flag.
  That is exactly the tacit rule this project removes.
- **Leave expired stock out of near-expiry value:** cleaner wording, but it
  hides the most urgent stock. Expired lots stay on the shelf until the
  monthly write-off, and the Responsible Pharmacist wants to see them first.

## Consequences
- `available_stock` is always below raw on-hand. Replenishment reorders a
  little earlier; the Head of Supply Chain accepted the extra working capital.
- Anyone who sums `on_hand_qty_base` directly gets a bigger, wrong number.
  The metric's `agent_notes` tell agents never to do that.
- Changing 90 to anything else changes two certified metrics and fails
  GQ-STOCK-01 to GQ-STOCK-03 until a new record supersedes this one.

## Worked example
Store NW001, SKU 100075 "Sodium chloride 0.9% nasal spray 70ml" (base unit:
bottle), snapshot 2026-09-15:

| Batch | Expires | Days to expiry | On hand | Value excl. VAT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B0000108 | 2026-11-25 | 71 | 1 | 59,437.92 |
| B0000109 | 2027-03-25 | 191 | 48 | 3,015,006.00 |

- No transfers in transit for this store and SKU.
- `available_stock` = 48 bottles (B0000108 is under 90 days).
- `near_expiry_value` = 59,437.92 VND.
- Under the rejected 60-day rule the answer would have been 49: this is the
  batch the two options disagree on.

Expired stock still counts as near-expiry value: store NW013, SKU 100149
"Compressor nebulizer", batch B0002679 expired on 2026-09-14
(`days_to_expiry` = -1) with 7 units on hand, so `near_expiry_value` for
that store and SKU is 4,896,318.00 VND, and `available_stock` counts none
of it.
