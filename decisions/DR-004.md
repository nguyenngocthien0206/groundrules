# DR-004: Local business day; lagging stores stay in, flagged

## Context
Two facts about POS data kept producing wrong daily numbers.

First, the vendors disagree on time. Vendor A stamps sales in UTC and cuts
its daily files on the UTC date; vendor B stamps local time. Vietnam is
UTC+7, so a sale at 02:00 in a 24-hour store is 19:00 the previous day in
UTC and arrives in the previous day's file. Reports built on file dates or
UTC dates moved night sales into the wrong day, and around month ends
into the wrong month.

Second, stores do not always sync. The two newest stores run on vendor B
and their sync has been failing for three days. Their sales for those days
have not reached any system. One report dropped them "to keep the trend
clean"; another kept them silently. Neither told the reader.

## Decision
1. Every date-based number uses the **local business day**
   (Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh), whatever the vendor stamps and whatever the file is
   called. Staging converts UTC to local; `business_date` is the only date
   to group sales by.
2. Stores whose POS sync lags **stay in every number**. They are never
   dropped, and they are never estimated. `dim_store.is_pos_sync_lagging`
   marks them, and any answer whose scope includes one must say which
   stores lag and up to which day their data runs. The answer carries the
   caveat `pos_sync_lag`.

## Options considered
- **Local day, keep and flag lagging stores (chosen):** numbers match what
  happened in the stores, and the reader learns what is missing.
- **Drop lagging stores until they catch up:** a smoother trend, but the
  chain total silently shrinks, and stock and cover for those stores look
  wrong without warning.
- **Estimate the missing days:** fills the gap, but turns a data problem
  into a made-up number that nobody can trace.
- **UTC or file date:** what the raw files suggest, and wrong for every
  night sale.

## Consequences
- Answers about recent days often carry a caveat. That is the point: the
  reader decides whether three missing days matter for the question.
- Lagging stores also look better stocked and have longer cover than they
  really do, because their ERP stock has not seen the unsynced sales
  (DR-010). The caveat covers stock and cover answers too.
- A per-store data test warns on every build while any store lags, so the
  data team sees it every morning.

## Worked example
Time zone: store NW012 is open 24 hours and runs vendor A. A sale at
**02:09:43 on 2026-08-01** local time is stamped `2026-07-31T19:09:43Z` and
arrives in vendor A's file `sales_20260731.csv`. Its receipt number
A0122608010003 carries the local date. Its `business_date` is
**2026-08-01**, so it counts in August, not July.

Lag: at the snapshot (extract on 2026-09-15 at 06:00), NW029 (opened
2026-08-18) and NW030 (opened 2026-09-01) last synced on 2026-09-12
around 05:30 with the error "connection timeout". Their data runs through
**2026-09-11**, so `pos_lag_days` = 3 for both. "Sales yesterday across
all stores" (2026-09-14) includes both stores with no sales, and the
answer must say: "NW029 and NW030 have no POS data after 2026-09-11."
