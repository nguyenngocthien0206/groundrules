# DR-005: Deprecate revenue_v1 in favor of net_revenue

## Context
`revenue_v1` was the first revenue number: what customers paid at the
till, VAT included, by sale date, with returns ignored. It was simple and
it matched the POS screens, so it spread into store reports and slide
decks. Once DR-002 and DR-006 settled how revenue is counted, it no
longer matched any number Finance reports.

Deleting it would not remove the habit. People and agents would still ask
for "revenue v1" or "till revenue" and get either nothing or a number
rebuilt by hand. A deprecated metric that answers with a pointer is safer
than a missing one.

## Decision
`revenue_v1` stays defined (`dbt/models/semantic/fct_sales.yml`) with
`status: deprecated` and `replaced_by: net_revenue`. It is not certified.
Agents and dashboards must not answer with it: they say it is deprecated,
name `net_revenue`, and explain the difference (VAT and returns).

## Options considered
- **Deprecate with a pointer (chosen):** old questions get a clear
  redirect, and the lifecycle is visible in the registry.
- **Delete it:** cleaner, but every old report breaks silently and people
  rebuild it in spreadsheets.
- **Keep it certified next to net_revenue:** two certified revenue numbers
  is how the conflicting dashboards started.

## Consequences
- The governance lint requires every deprecated metric to point to a
  certified replacement.
- Old dashboards built on it are listed as exposures and move to
  `net_revenue` over the next quarter.
- Its number stays queryable for audits, which is why it is deprecated and
  not hidden.

## Worked example
Whole chain, August 2026:
- `revenue_v1` = sum of amounts paid, VAT included = **7,929,135,600 VND**.
- Sales after discounts, before VAT: 7,442,338,601.72 VND. Refunds before
  VAT, returned in August: 68,895,839.13 VND.
- `net_revenue` = 7,442,338,601.72 - 68,895,839.13 = **7,373,442,762.59 VND**.
- The gap, 555,692,837.41 VND (about 7%), is VAT plus August's returns.
  Anyone still quoting the old number overstates revenue by that much.
