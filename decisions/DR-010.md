# DR-010: Days of cover uses the pace of the 28 full days before the snapshot

## Context
Days of cover answers "how long will this stock last?": available stock
divided by how fast it sells. Everyone agreed on the numerator (DR-001,
DR-003, DR-007). The fight was about "how fast". A short window reacts
to every promotion and every rainy week; a long one still remembers the
dry season when cough syrup barely moved.

There was also a quieter mistake. The snapshot is taken in the early
morning, before the day's sales exist. A window that ends on the snapshot
day includes one empty day and understates the pace.

## Decision
`days_of_cover` = `available_stock` at the snapshot divided by the average
daily base units sold over the **28 full days before the snapshot day**
(for the 2026-09-15 snapshot: 2026-08-18 to 2026-09-14). In
`dbt/models/semantic/metrics.yml` this is a 28-day cumulative window of
`units_sold`, offset by one day.

## Options considered
- **28 full days (chosen):** four full weeks, so each weekday counts
  four times and weekend peaks do not bias it.
- **7 days:** reacts fastest, but one promotion or one rainy week swings
  the answer by half.
- **90 days:** smooth, but in September it still averages in July, before
  the cough and cold season picked up.
- **28 days ending on the snapshot day:** what the first version did. It
  includes the snapshot day, which has no sales yet.

## Consequences
- Answers should be asked at the snapshot date; the metric needs a day
  (`metric_time`) to anchor the window.
- Stores open less than 28 days, or whose POS sync lags (DR-004), show too
  few recent sales and so too many days of cover. Answers that include
  them must say so.
- A promotion in the last four weeks shortens cover for a month after it.

## Worked example
Store NW001, snapshot 2026-09-15:
- Long-dated on-hand (DR-001): 40,282 base units. Outbound in transit
  (DR-003): 48. `available_stock` = 40,282 - 48 = 40,234.
- Base units sold from 2026-08-18 to 2026-09-14: 42,803, so the pace is
  42,803 / 28 = 1,528.68 per day.
- `days_of_cover` = 40,234 / 1,528.68 = **26.32 days**.

The lag caveat in practice: NW029 opened on 2026-08-18 and its sales stop
at 2026-09-11. Its window holds only 25 days of sales divided by 28, so its
cover reads 38.8 days, longer than it really is.
